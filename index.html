FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about dental visits, oral hygiene, and treatment care at Vimak Dental Centre.

In general, visiting the dentist every 6 months for a routine examination and professional cleaning helps maintain healthy teeth and gums. However, the ideal check-up schedule depends on your individual oral health condition. Your dentist will recommend a follow-up interval suited to your needs after assessing your teeth and gums.

During your initial visit at Vimak Dental Centre, the dentist will listen to your concerns, review your dental and medical history, and perform a gentle clinical examination of your teeth and gums. Where necessary, diagnostic X-rays may be recommended. Your dentist will then explain their findings clearly and discuss suitable treatment options before any procedure is carried out.

A root canal treatment may be recommended when the soft inner tissue (pulp) of a tooth becomes inflamed or infected due to deep decay, repeated dental procedures, or a crack or injury to the tooth. Common signs include persistent toothache, prolonged sensitivity to hot or cold, tenderness when chewing, or gum swelling. Because symptoms vary, a dental professional must examine the tooth and take an X-ray to confirm whether root canal therapy is appropriate.

Start cleaning your child's teeth as soon as the first tooth appears, supervise brushing twice daily with an age-appropriate fluoride toothpaste, and limit sugary snacks and drinks between meals. Regular paediatric dental check-ups allow a dentist to monitor tooth development, apply preventive treatments where helpful, and help your child build positive, stress-free dental habits.

Because every patient's oral health needs are unique, treatment plans and costs depend on a clinical assessment by a dentist. Please contact our Nairobi or Thika clinic via phone or WhatsApp, or book a consultation so our team can assess your needs and provide transparent pricing information.

How Often Should You Visit the Dentist?

Routine dental check-ups are an essential part of preventive healthcare. For most adults and children, visiting the dentist every six months allows your dental professional to:

  • Check for early signs of tooth decay before cavities cause pain.
  • Assess gum health and remove hardened plaque (tartar/calculus) through professional scaling and polishing.
  • Monitor children's developing teeth and bite alignment.
  • Provide personalized guidance on brushing, flossing, and diet.

Clinical Note: Depending on your oral health history, gum condition, or ongoing treatments, your dentist may recommend more frequent visits. Always consult a dental professional for an individual assessment.

What Is a Root Canal Treatment?

A root canal treatment (endodontic therapy) is a dental procedure used to treat infection or irreversible inflammation at the centre of a tooth (the dental pulp).

  • Why it is done: When deep tooth decay, a crack, or trauma reaches the inner pulp, bacteria can cause pain, sensitivity, or swelling.
  • How it works: The dentist carefully removes the infected pulp tissue, cleans and shapes the root canals, and seals the space with a biocompatible material.
  • Restoring the tooth: Afterward, a filling or dental crown is typically placed to protect the tooth and restore normal chewing function.

Clinical Note: Only a clinical examination and dental X-ray can determine whether a tooth requires a filling, root canal treatment, or another solution.

How Can You Protect Your Child's Teeth?

Healthy primary (milk) teeth help children chew comfortably, speak clearly, and hold space for permanent teeth. Parents can support their child's smile by:

  • Supervising brushing twice a day (morning and before bed) using an age-appropriate fluoride toothpaste.
  • Limiting frequent sugary snacks, sweets, and sweetened drinks between meals.
  • Bringing children for regular dental check-ups so they become familiar and comfortable with the clinic environment.
  • Asking your dentist about preventive options such as topical fluoride or fissure sealants.

Clinical Note: A paediatric dental check-up allows our team to give tailored advice for your child's age and dental development stage.

Why Regular Dental Checkups Matter

Many dental conditions, such as early cavities or gum disease, develop gradually without immediate pain. Regular dental visits help you stay ahead of dental problems:

  • Preventive Cleaning: Professional scaling and polishing remove plaque and tartar buildup along the gumline.
  • Early Detection: Identifying small issues early often means simpler, more conservative dental treatment.
  • Confidence & Comfort: Maintaining clean teeth and healthy gums supports fresh breath and a confident smile.
